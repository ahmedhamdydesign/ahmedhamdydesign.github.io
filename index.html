01 · Graphic novel, printed

The Drifter

A 28-page crime graphic novel, adapted from a motion comic. I designed the book: panel layout, all the lettering, the title logo, the part dividers, and the print files.

Halfway through, the client switched printers from KDP to Mixam. I rebuilt the cover to the new spec, with the spine sized to the page count and the paper stock. When the editor's notes came back I reworked the internal monologue into floating thought bubbles, cut a confusing exchange, and re-pointed a line of dialogue to the sister who actually said it.

My role: book design, lettering, title logo, editorial revisions, print production. Interior artwork supplied by the client.

02 · Book covers

A cover has to sell the story in one look

Cover art for children's picture books. Each one is built to survive two very different tests: it has to read instantly as a small thumbnail on a store page, and it has to hold up as a printed jacket at full size.

That means the title lettering is drawn, not typed, the focal character sits where the eye lands first, and the palette stays readable when the whole thing is two centimetres tall.

Note: several of these were drawn as work for hire and published under the publisher's credited name.

03 · Character design

From turnaround sheet to final paint

A character is not one drawing. It is a face that stays the same across forty pages, a silhouette you can recognise at a distance, and a set of expressions the writer can actually use.

So I start with a development sheet: the character from several angles, in line, before any colour goes down. The client approves the shape first. Then I paint.

04 · Interiors and scenes

The pages between the covers

Full-bleed spreads, environment work, and the layout that holds the text. I set the type inside the artwork so the words sit in the quiet part of the picture instead of fighting it.

05 · Process

This is what "no AI" actually looks like

Anyone can say they do not use AI. Here is the proof: the pencil stage. Every job starts as a rough storyboard, panel by panel, with the text already placed so the client can see the pacing before a single colour is chosen.

You approve the sketch. Then I paint it. And I can send you the screen recording of the whole thing being drawn if you ever want to see it.

  1. 01

    Rough

    Pencil thumbnails and a storyboard with the text placed. You see the pacing and the composition before anything is final.

  2. 02

    Line and colour

    Clean line art, then paint. Two rounds of revisions are built into the price, not billed on top.

  3. 03

    Print-ready files

    Layered source, flattened exports, correct bleed, and a spine measured to your page count and paper stock.
